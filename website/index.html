How it works

Email is the network. Encryption is the default.

@me turns your inbox into a private delivery channel — then hides every trace of it from your regular mail.

  1. 01

    Sign in with your email

    Type your address and approve with your provider. Your encryption keys are created on your device and never leave it.

  2. 02

    Message anyone by address

    Each message is encrypted for the recipient, signed by you, and sent through your provider like any email — only it's unreadable to everyone else.

  3. 03

    It feels like texting

    @me filters out normal email and shows only your conversations — threads, reactions, voice notes, and read receipts.

What we can't promise — and won't pretend to

  • Metadata: your email provider can see that you emailed someone and when — never what you said. Burner aliases reduce this.
  • Screenshots: no app can stop someone photographing their screen. Shredding is enforced by verified apps, and we show you which ones are.
  • Delivery speed follows your provider — usually a few seconds.
  • Web Updates: Because @me installs via the web, security patches arrive instantaneously through service workers with zero App Store review lag.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

Can Google, Microsoft, or my provider read my messages?

No. Messages are encrypted on your device before they're sent. Your provider only stores and delivers scrambled ciphertext it has no cryptographic key for.

Why isn't @me in the Apple App Store or Google Play Store?

By operating as an installable Progressive Web App (PWA), @me completely eliminates app store tracking, gatekeeper censorship, and 30% taxes. You install it in two taps directly from Safari, Chrome, or Edge to your home screen or dock. It runs in its own window with biometric FaceID/TouchID lock, offline caching, and push alerts without any Apple or Google store telemetry.

Will this clutter my inbox?

No. @me messages are tagged and filed away from your normal mail, and the app strictly ignores every regular email. Your inbox stays your inbox.

What if the person I message doesn't have @me yet?

They receive a sealed invite email with an "Open & Decrypt" button. They don't need to visit an app store or download a binary — they approve with their Google or Microsoft account in 1 tap, their browser generates keys on their device, and your conversation begins encrypted immediately.

Where are my messages stored?

Encrypted on your devices and, as ciphertext, in your own mailbox. There's no @me central database — we couldn't hand your messages over if we were subpoenaed.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Lock your account remotely and trigger remote key shredding from another browser to wipe your threads and keys from the lost device.

Is it free?

Yes. @me runs on the email infrastructure you already have, so there are zero relay servers for us to bill you for.
